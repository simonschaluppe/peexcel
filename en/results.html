Results and indicators

Where in PEExcel? 📊 Erg (Ergebnisse).

This sheet contains tables and charts for the currently calculated variant. It is distinct from the internal OUT results store and the Excel dashboard for time-resolved analysis.

Available indicators

Browse all OUT variables: units, labels and assignments from the active schema, including costs, flexibility and storage.

Annual results complement hourly charts. Keep indicator, reference area and balance boundary consistent when comparing.

Area Indicators Interpretation
Buildings Heating/cooling demand, heat gains and losses Distinguish annual energy from power and temperature
Electricity Demand, grid import, PV use and feed-in Read local supply separately from external mobility charging
PV self-consumption Direct, flexible and total shares Share of PV generation assigned to the respective local uses
Energy autonomy Local direct and flexible PV use relative to electricity demand A PV-related indicator, not supply security or islanding capability
Grid feed-in Peak PV export in kW and its timestamp Maximum on an hourly basis; no statement about shorter peaks or grid voltages
Stationary battery Annual charge/discharge, losses, round-trip efficiency, equivalent full cycles Assess use and losses together; full cycles alone do not determine service life
Flexibility Annual and summer/winter enabled-time shares, flexible grid and PV use Enablement is an opportunity; actual use depends on demand and storage state
Mobility Distance travelled, electric/fossil destination-traffic demand, EV charging and full cycles Generated traffic and physical charging have different boundaries
Assessment Primary energy, operational/embodied GHG contributions, total balance and distance to budget Assessed quantities complement physical flows

Self-consumption and energy autonomy

The PV self-consumption share divides direct plus flexible local PV use by PV generation. Energy autonomy uses the same numerator but total electricity demand as denominator. Higher PV yield can affect these two shares differently.

When comparing tools, align definitions, including mobility and storage charging. The same term, such as “self-sufficiency”, does not guarantee the same boundary.

Project indicators

Check project/variant name, areas, use mix, development density, and cooled and mechanically ventilated shares. Note whether each balance refers to net floor area (NGF) or gross floor area (BGF).

Heat balance

Charts compare transmission and ventilation losses, night ventilation, solar and internal gains, and heating/cooling demand. Read the whole district, cooled and uncooled areas, and heating/cooling periods separately. Heating and cooling capacities are power quantities, not annual energy.

Final-energy balance

The demand–supply matrix assigns user electricity, building services, hot water, storage charging and mobility to supply sources. Row and column sums make energy use traceable. Read imports, local use, storage flows and feed-in within their boundaries; do not indiscriminately add them.

Flexibility and battery

Enabled periods, flexible use, battery charge/discharge, losses and cycles describe how available potential is used. Annual indicators complement the dashboard’s time-resolved analysis.

Primary-energy balance

Demand and supply are assessed using conversion factors. Local renewables and flexible imports appear as substitution equivalents; context factors account for development density, refurbishment and mobility. The import/export presentation covers exchange across the district boundary. Read each presentation using its own signs and system boundary.

GHG balance

Construction and operational contributions, including mobility, are shown cumulatively. Emissions, credits, budget and balance are separate quantities. Check assessment period and gross-floor-area reference. LCA inputs and verification are documented separately.

Common concept-design questions

Are variant comparison and sensitivity studies already available?

Yes. Save inputs as variants, calculate individually or by batch, and evaluate in variant comparison. For example, compare storage sizes and C-rates with the same building and PV system. Variants are deliberately specified; this is not automatic optimisation.

What time resolution is available?

The annual simulation uses 8,760 hours. Daily, monthly and annual evaluations aggregate these results. A 15-minute model would require adapted input data and calculation methods; splitting an hourly value into four equal intervals adds no temporal information.

Can grid relief be assessed?

Hourly import/export profiles and peak feed-in support comparison of district exchange. Transformer loading, voltage limits and individual feeders require an additional grid model. Lower annual imports alone do not demonstrate relief at the critical grid hour.

How are CO₂ savings determined?

Compare a reference and a measure variant with the same assessment boundary. Distinguish operational and life-cycle balances, using the same factors, periods and areas. If factors or signals change, report their effects explicitly, as in the flexibility example.

Are economics, resilience and permitting covered?

The results structure contains cost fields for grid import, flexible import and feed-in. This does not establish a complete investment or life-cycle cost analysis. Check tariff assumptions and output completeness for economic evaluation. Grid-outage resilience, fire safety and permitting require additional criteria and studies.

What data does a use case need?

Start with location, areas and uses, envelope properties, supply, PV, storage and mobility assumptions. Presets support initial concepts; replace key assumptions with project-specific data afterwards. Comparison with operation benefits from time-aligned measurements of consumption, generation, grid exchange and, ideally, storage states. Data interfaces distinguish concept and validation data.
